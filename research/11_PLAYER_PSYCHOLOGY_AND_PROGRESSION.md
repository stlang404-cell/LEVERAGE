# Motivation without compulsory risk or waiting

Design hypotheses, not retention research findings. Mastery offers teachable tactics and post-match review; rivalry preserves named opponents and contextual history; status distinguishes competitive, leadership and investor achievements so one path does not invalidate another. No daily login bonuses, real-time timers, loot boxes or monetized recovery. Advance time deliberately; permit pausing before a consequential choice.

A 5–10 minute session target: one match or one meaningful staffing/investment decision, then a durable save. Longer sessions support tournament brackets, recruitment and ownership strategy. Match duration has not been human-tested; lab plies cannot be converted into minutes without timing studies.

Every setback offers at least two affordable actions: practice/review, apply to another vacancy, public league matches, paid sponsor exhibition, train or divest an asset. Bankruptcy never locks the board. Optional simplified management delegates decisions while preserving budget logs. Board-only mode is a complete mode, not a tutorial for betting.

Milestones grant new verbs: team roster control, departmental restructuring, corporate capital allocation and shareholder votes. Avoid a thousand-step grade ladder: thousands of actual positions, perhaps 8 authority bands. Rival reactions follow observed events and personality; avoid repeated canned hostility. A loss to a rival can open a rematch, a transfer offer or a new training plan.

Validation: observe 12 testers teaching rules back, returning voluntarily for a second session, naming one meaningful positional choice and one recovery path. Collect reasons for quitting without coercive retention targets. Compare administrative time to match/review time; if mandatory administration dominates, simplify. Competitive satisfaction, loss tolerance and investor-only enjoyment remain unknown until playtests.
