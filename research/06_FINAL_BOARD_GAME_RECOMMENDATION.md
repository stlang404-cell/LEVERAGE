# Conditional recommendation, not a production greenlight

Leverage is the **research lead** because structural collapse best expresses the corporate network fantasy. Relay is the fallback for the first human-facing prototype because its current experiments have fewer draws and clear movement. Neither is a definitive winner. Do not market strategic depth, fairness, uniqueness or strong AI as proven.

## Weighted framework

Weights sum to 100: depth 18, simplicity 12, originality 10, fairness 12, AI feasibility 10, spectator excitement 7, tactical richness 8, positional richness 8, replayability 5, visual clarity 5, tournament integration 3, prediction-market suitability 2. Markets are deliberately low weight: they must serve the board game. Fairness and depth remain unknown, so no fabricated total fills those cells.

Scores 1–5 are designer hypotheses, not measured preferences. Since unknown weight is 35, an overall ranking would hide too much uncertainty. Even originality scores express confidence only; no comprehensive prior-art search occurred.

Proceed to a disposable interactive Leverage v0.1 prototype with transparent draw behavior, not a premium production build. Resolve the connectivity variants in 05, compare stronger paired agents and run human trials. Promote one ruleset to v1 only after clear teaching, nontrivial repeated play and no known repeatable opening exploit. If no candidate passes, continue research. Choosing to stop here is preferable to implementing a large corporation around a weak core.

## Executed connectivity follow-up

A separate 96-game experiment tested strict resulting connectivity (48/48 draws) and own-piece cleanup after every action (29/48 draws). Both performed worse on sampled draw frequency and are rejected as default fixes. These variants do not modify or replace v0.1. See 04 and `results/variants.json`. More research remains necessary before a production rules freeze.
